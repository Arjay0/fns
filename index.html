Fruit & Swords · Host Guide
Based on a successful first session ✓

At a Glance

  • FNS is a tabletop RPG created by kids, for kids. Think D&D, but your own way.
  • Group size: 5–7 players worked great.
  • Session length: ~2.5–3 hours total (gaming + breaks + activity).
  • Each player brings their own character binder and dice set. Remind them ahead of time!
  • At least one parent/adult should be nearby during active play.

Before They Arrive

  • Put a tablecloth down. Clay (or whatever hand activity you pick) gets everywhere.
  • Set a paper plate at each seat as a personal work surface.
  • Set out snacks in bowls in the middle of the table before they walk in.
  • Remind guests to bring their character binder and dice set when you confirm attendance. One player forgot his binder and had to go get it.

Session Flow

Arrival (0:00–0:30)
Kids trickle in. Give them something to do with their hands while they wait for everyone to show up. No awkward standing around, and it warms them up for the session.
Round 1 (0:30–1:00)
Start FNS once everyone's settled. Expect a little distraction from the hand activity at first — that's fine. They'll find their rhythm. Each round = ~30 min, ~4–5 turns per player.
Break (1:00–1:05)
Short 5-minute break. Bathroom, snacks, talk. Keep it brief so momentum doesn't die.
Round 2 (1:05–1:35)
Back to FNS for another 30 minutes. By now the group is in a groove. Another ~4–5 turns each.
Activity Break (1:35–2:05)
Natural transition point — everyone stands up and does something active. Plan this as a dedicated block ahead of time (and let the kids know it's coming). Leaving it open-ended creates debate about when to switch. What the activity is is up to you and what you have available.
Wind Down (2:05–2:30)
One more short activity burst or back to FNS, then cool down. Kids help clean up and pack up binders. Air clay cleanup is pretty easy — mostly big chunks.

What Worked Really Well

  • Giving kids something to do with their hands between turns was huge. It kept the energy up without chaos. We used air clay — your setup may vary, but something in this category is worth planning for.
  • Toothpicks and popsicle sticks were used as sculpting tools. Fun bonus if you go the clay route.
  • Character creation via clay was a natural fit — most kids made their FNS character out of clay during the session.

Things to Watch Out For

  • No open cups of water. One spilled and made a mess with the clay. Closed bottles only.
  • Dice rolling area can get crowded. Consider a small tray in the center of the table as a designated roll spot.
  • Mixed clay colors actually look cool. Instead of tossing combined scraps, bag them up — the clay can be reused and the blended colors are a bonus.
  • Plan breaks ahead of time and tell the kids the structure at the start. "After round 2 we'll take a break and do something active" goes a long way toward keeping transitions smooth.

Ideas for Next Time

  • Add fidget toys alongside (or instead of) clay for downtime.
  • Remind guests to bring their binder and dice in the day-before message.
  • Chips are a safe snack bet.
  • A dedicated activity hour (vs. squeezing it between rounds) might let kids go deeper into both FNS and the break activity.
